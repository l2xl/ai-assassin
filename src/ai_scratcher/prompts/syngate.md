# Syngate DAG rules
The syngate items form a tree or, in the general case, a directed acyclic graph (DAG): a condensed record of the analysis and synthesis of the project, which sets the abstraction level and the scope of the AI context, the test coverage and the sustainability a human-controlled review provides. The seed context of an exchange anchored at an item is the chain of its ancestors' statements, root first, then its own, then those of all its descendants in depth, each item once. For the process to stay effective the items follow these rules:
- Every leaf is one simple testable statement about the project, read as a requirement. A statement that is not testable as a whole is split into children, each testable or split again.
- Complex behavioural design descriptions are kept out of the items, in the project's text or markdown files, and the item refers to such a file by a root-relative link. The design file usually referenced by the feature branch item and describes the whole feature behaviour, decomposition rules, guidelines, etc., then the branch item children defines exact testable requirements. The testable requirements covers important and strictly testable desisions and the design file holds the idea in generai and definitions may be hard to define tests for.
- The project's tests bind to exact items by tags equal to the item UID. No item is created just to refer to a design document or to describe the tests of its parent.
- An intermediate branch exists only for a statement of its own that its children's seed context needs. A branch that merely groups its children under a heading is not created: they go directly under its parent. A description restating the parent's — such as a link to the same design document the parent already links to — marks exactly such a branch and is never added.
- An item's statement is one markdown text, read as a section of one document under its ancestors and detailed by its children. Its first line is the statement condensed — the whole requirement where one sentence holds it, the one "shall" of a test-bearing leaf, or a feature's bare name where its children carry the content — and the outline shows it as the title. The lines past one blank line hold only what the first cannot: enumerated cases, limits, exceptions, a design-file link. They never paraphrase the first line, and no statement repeats its parent's or its children's.
- Whoever adds or rewords an item rereads the whole branch — the parent and every descendant — and strips what the change made redundant, so the compiled text reads seamlessly.
- An item lists its key source files in `sources`, root-relative, so they enter the seed context at it, above and below it; a file listed by an ancestor is not listed again.
- A whole feature is named without a number; its sub-items carry the feature's name with a numbered suffix, unless one of them is a feature itself again.
